# Follow-up (same day) — the size ceilings come out of the brief

## The request

Three prompts, escalating. First: what's missing between `blockchain-systems` and
`defi-and-onchain-economics`. Second: which of those gaps fit *inside* the two
existing pages. Third, after I noted both pages sat at exactly the stated limits:

> why is there is a contract band of 14 and 50? how were these arbitrary no
> selected? quality coverage should not be based on an arbitrary no but on the
> rightful coverage needed. there can be a floor but not a ceiling.

Correct, and the repo's own record already said so. I did not choose those
numbers — they were in `CLAUDE.md` from the start — but I had been treating them
as binding when suggesting additions, which is how the question came up.

## The evidence the ceilings were wrong

- Word ceiling was 5,000. Actual: ~9,160 (information-theory, since removed),
  8,510 (blockchain-systems), 9,188 (defi-and-onchain-economics). Three for three
  over, each one flagged in a report and then ignored.
- Resource ceiling was 50. Both crypto pages: exactly 50. That is not what a
  non-binding guideline looks like.
- `LEARNINGS.md` had carried an open note — "the depth bar and the word range are
  in tension" — since 2026-08-03, and `memory/2026-08-03.md` explicitly deferred
  it: "worth a decision next time whether the guide is a target or a floor."

A ceiling that every honest page violates is measuring the wrong thing. The band
was also doing real damage in the direction nobody notices: it makes the correct
move on a large field — cover it fully — look like a rule violation, so coverage
gets rationed to protect a number.

## What changed

**`CLAUDE.md`**

- The trailing length paragraph under *The depth bar* is now its own section,
  **Size: floors, not ceilings**. Floors kept and stated as floors (8 modules,
  ~2,500 words). Maxima gone.
- Bloat is now caught by four quality tests, all of which apply at any page
  length: every module passes the dependency test, no two modules overlap,
  nothing is inflated to look thorough, the rail stays navigable. Each has a fix
  that is never "cut to hit a number".
- A blockquote records why the band was removed, so a future session doesn't
  helpfully reinstate it.
- Resource rule: **at least 25, no upper limit**. The guard against an unfiltered
  list is the existing machinery — every entry cited by a module, every entry
  carrying a real note — not a cap.
- *Don't pad* now cuts both ways: dropping a load-bearing text because the list
  already looks long is the same error mirrored.
- *Updating an existing topic* gains a paragraph: growing a page is a normal
  update, new modules append, and if a new module belongs earlier you renumber
  and fix every `#mN` and `r-used` rather than parking it out of dependency order.
- Two anti-patterns added: stopping short because the page "feels long", and
  reporting a page complete while knowing a load-bearing part is missing.
- Pre-flight gains: no load-bearing part of the field missing, and deliberate
  omissions named in the report.

**`LEARNINGS.md`** — the open "page-length guidance is loose" note is now
"Page size has floors and no ceilings — resolved 2026-08-06", carrying the three
word counts and the 50/50 resource tell as the evidence.

## Consequence for the two crypto pages

The additions I proposed no longer have to displace anything. Both pages can grow
past 13 modules and past 50 resources when the work is done. Nothing on either
page changed in this session — the brief changed, not the pages.

## Not done

No page edits. The suggested modules — privacy and custody on `blockchain-systems`,
derivatives and the yield stack on `defi-and-onchain-economics` — are still just
suggestions.

# 2026-08-06 (later) — Information Theory, rebuilt from scratch

## The request

"add a syllabus for information theory." Then, mid-planning: "Instead of next,
give quantum information an additional module."

The interesting context is the first entry in this same file: the topic had been
deleted earlier the same day, at `0f7e79b`, as a deliberate walk of the removal
path. Two commits after that, `40333ae` replaced the "8–14 modules / 2,500–5,000
words / 50 resources" band with floors and no ceilings. Both facts argued against
`git revert`: the deleted page stopped at exactly 13 modules and exactly 50
resources because the ceiling told it to, and its links were a year stale. So the
slug came back at the same URL with entirely new content.

## Shipped

`docs/information-theory/` — MA-01, **21 modules, 94 resources, ~16,800 words**
of syllabus body, ≈160 h, Beginner → Advanced. For comparison the two crypto
pages are 13/50 and 8.5–9.2k words; this one is larger because it has 21 modules,
and per-module it is comparable (≈800 words versus ≈650).

Two other files changed. `docs/index.html` regained a **Mathematics &amp;
statistics** `.cat-group` — deleted this morning when it emptied — in alphabetical
position after *Economics &amp; finance*. And `docs/blockchain-systems/index.html`
had its `next` entry for information theory turned back from plain `<strong>` into
a link; the earlier entry in this file records demoting it, and leaving it flat
would have shipped the new page with no inbound link from anywhere but the
catalog.

## The arc, and why it is in this order

Five gears, then the contrast module:

- **Problem** — M01, the pre-1948 belief that reliability costs rate, installed
  deliberately so M08 lands as a shock.
- **Foundations** — M02 entropy from axioms *and* from codelength; M03 divergence
  and mutual information, taught as the primitives rather than as derived
  curiosities, because every converse on the page is a DPI; M04 differential
  entropy and maximum entropy, which exists mainly as inoculation — this is where
  applied misuse originates; M05 AEP and typical sets, the engine both coding
  theorems run on.
- **Compression** — M06 Kraft through Huffman, arithmetic coding and ANS; M07
  universality, MDL, Kolmogorov, and the compressor↔predictor equivalence that
  M17 later needs.
- **Channels** — M08 capacity and random coding; M09 Gaussian and waterfilling;
  M10 error exponents and finite blocklength, which is the module a first course
  omits and a practitioner most needs; M11 algebraic coding; M12 turbo/LDPC/polar
  and the 5G split; M13 rate–distortion as M08's dual.
- **Beyond point-to-point** — M14 networks and the fifty-year-open problems; M15
  secrecy, leakage and privacy; M16 information theory as a statistical tool;
  M17 machine learning; M18 estimation, which is what stops M17 being vocabulary;
  M19 physics/biology/betting, the page's one elective.
- **M20 quantum**, **M21 frontier.**

M18 is the placement I would defend hardest. It sits *after* M17 rather than
before, because its motivation only bites once the reader has seen mutual
information used as an objective — and it is the module that makes the M17 trap
actionable rather than merely cautionary.

## The quantum module

The follow-up request converted a `next` pointer into M20 (≈10 h, the
second-longest module). It is placed after the whole classical arc rather than
beside M08 on purpose: every result in it is the deliberate mirror of a classical
theorem the reader has already proved, so the module is structured as "here is
what carries over and here is what breaks". Schumacher compression is M06 with a
typical subspace; HSW is M08 with a regularisation that provably cannot be
dropped; stabiliser and CSS codes are M11's parity checks lifted; BB84 is M15's
two-stage post-processing. The genuinely alien result is **superactivation**
(Smith &amp; Yard 2008) — two zero-capacity channels combining to positive quantum
capacity, which has no classical analogue at all and is the reason the module is
not "classical theory with new symbols".

Quantum *computation* — Shor, Grover, fault-tolerance engineering — stayed out
and is named in `next`. The module says so in its own text.

## The one real bug

14 px of horizontal scroll at 380 px, traced to an unclassed `LI` at 374 > 340.
`.assigned li` is `display: flex`, so each inline child is a flex item and the
line's minimum width is the sum of the items' min-contents. Four work-through
items had **two** citations each — five flex items instead of three. Splitting
each into two `<li>`s fixed it, matches the pattern every existing page already
follows, and needed no stylesheet change. Written up in `LEARNINGS.md`.

## Deliberate omissions, stated

- Quantum computation, as above.
- Coding theory as pure mathematics — algebraic-geometry codes, MacWilliams
  identities, list decoding past a mention. M11 covers what an engineer needs.
- Signal processing and modulation — constellations, equalisation, OFDM. It sits
  under the channel model, not inside information theory.

## Thin spot

**Talks &amp; video is three entries**, which is the weakest group on the page. The
field's best teaching is written, and MacKay's sixteen lectures — the one
substantial video resource — sit in Courses because the group is named "Courses
&amp; lecture series". Worth revisiting if a good lecture on polar codes or on
finite blocklength turns up.
