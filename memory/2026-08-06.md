# 2026-08-06 — Removed the Information Theory topic

## The request

"remove the information theory topic."

No ambiguity, so no clarifying question. The only judgement calls were about
blast radius: what else in the repo assumes the page exists, and which of those
references are live site content versus historical record.

## What was removed

- **`docs/information-theory/index.html`** — the whole folder (MA-01, 13 modules,
  50 resources, shipped 2026-08-03). It was the repo's first topic page.
- **The catalog card** in `docs/index.html`, and with it the entire
  `Mathematics &amp; statistics` `.cat-group` — it held no other topic, and a
  category heading above an empty `card-list` looks like a bug rather than a
  category waiting to be filled. Catalog is now CS-01 and EF-01. The
  `CATALOG:START` / `CATALOG:END` markers and the how-to-add comment are intact,
  so the next Mathematics topic re-adds the group in alphabetical position.

## The non-obvious reference

`docs/blockchain-systems/index.html:975` linked to `../information-theory/` from
its `next` section — erasure coding as the honest treatment of what a
Reed–Solomon commitment does. Cross-page links live only in `next`, and the
pre-flight greps in `CLAUDE.md` are all intra-page (`#r-NN` vs `id="r-NN"`,
modules vs rail), so nothing in the existing checklist would have caught it.

Demoted rather than deleted: the entry is now plain `<strong>Information theory
and coding</strong>` pointing at the literature instead of at a dead path. The
same shape as the already-unlinked `Distributed systems` and `Applied
cryptography` entries beside it, which is the pattern for an adjacent topic the
library does not cover yet. If the topic is ever re-added, that line becomes a
link again.

## README

The "Adding a topic" section used `information theory` as its worked example,
down to `docs/information-theory/index.html` as the output path. Left alone it

## What was deliberately left alone

`PROMPT.md`, `LEARNINGS.md` and `memory/2026-08-03.md` / `2026-08-05.md` all
discuss the information-theory page. They are records of decisions actually
taken — the *A Mind at Play* cut and the "Inaccessible ≠ unciteable" rule that
came out of it, the audit script validated against the page before it was trusted
on the new ones. Scrubbing the topic from them would erase the reasoning that
produced live rules in `CLAUDE.md`.

# Follow-up (same day) — the size ceilings come out of the brief

## The request

Three prompts, escalating. First: what's missing between `blockchain-systems` and
`defi-and-onchain-economics`. Second: which of those gaps fit *inside* the two
existing pages. Third, after I noted both pages sat at exactly the stated limits:

> why is there is a contract band of 14 and 50? how were these arbitrary no
> selected? quality coverage should not be based on an arbitrary no but on the
> rightful coverage needed. there can be a floor but not a ceiling.

Correct, and the repo's own record already said so. I did not choose those
numbers — they were in `CLAUDE.md` from the start — but I had been treating them
as binding when suggesting additions, which is how the question came up.

## The evidence the ceilings were wrong

- Word ceiling was 5,000. Actual: ~9,160 (information-theory, since removed),
  8,510 (blockchain-systems), 9,188 (defi-and-onchain-economics). Three for three
  over, each one flagged in a report and then ignored.
- Resource ceiling was 50. Both crypto pages: exactly 50. That is not what a
  non-binding guideline looks like.
- `LEARNINGS.md` had carried an open note — "the depth bar and the word range are
  in tension" — since 2026-08-03, and `memory/2026-08-03.md` explicitly deferred
  it: "worth a decision next time whether the guide is a target or a floor."

A ceiling that every honest page violates is measuring the wrong thing. The band
was also doing real damage in the direction nobody notices: it makes the correct
move on a large field — cover it fully — look like a rule violation, so coverage
gets rationed to protect a number.

## What changed

**`CLAUDE.md`**

- The trailing length paragraph under *The depth bar* is now its own section,
  **Size: floors, not ceilings**. Floors kept and stated as floors (8 modules,
  ~2,500 words). Maxima gone.
- Bloat is now caught by four quality tests, all of which apply at any page
  length: every module passes the dependency test, no two modules overlap,
  nothing is inflated to look thorough, the rail stays navigable. Each has a fix
  that is never "cut to hit a number".
- A blockquote records why the band was removed, so a future session doesn't
  helpfully reinstate it.
- Resource rule: **at least 25, no upper limit**. The guard against an unfiltered
  list is the existing machinery — every entry cited by a module, every entry
  carrying a real note — not a cap.
- *Don't pad* now cuts both ways: dropping a load-bearing text because the list
  already looks long is the same error mirrored.
- *Updating an existing topic* gains a paragraph: growing a page is a normal
  update, new modules append, and if a new module belongs earlier you renumber
  and fix every `#mN` and `r-used` rather than parking it out of dependency order.
- Two anti-patterns added: stopping short because the page "feels long", and
  reporting a page complete while knowing a load-bearing part is missing.
- Pre-flight gains: no load-bearing part of the field missing, and deliberate
  omissions named in the report.

**`LEARNINGS.md`** — the open "page-length guidance is loose" note is now
"Page size has floors and no ceilings — resolved 2026-08-06", carrying the three
word counts and the 50/50 resource tell as the evidence.

## Consequence for the two crypto pages

The additions I proposed no longer have to displace anything. Both pages can grow
past 13 modules and past 50 resources when the work is done. Nothing on either
page changed in this session — the brief changed, not the pages.

## Not done

No page edits. The suggested modules — privacy and custody on `blockchain-systems`,
derivatives and the yield stack on `defi-and-onchain-economics` — are still just
suggestions.
